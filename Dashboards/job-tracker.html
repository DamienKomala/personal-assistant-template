03 — Return on effort

Which sources produce a human, and which companies have received more applications than they've ever answered.

Companies applied to more than once

Counted across all three ledger files, grouping names after normalizing legal suffixes and parentheticals. These numbers undercount and are meant to. The ledger holds what was tracked; the real repeat check is a Gmail search for in:anywhere "<company>", which covers every application ever made. A company showing twice here may have been applied to ten times.

Sources that reach a human

Reached a human means the row got past submitted — a reply, a screen, an interview, or a rejection someone actually sent. A row that simply went quiet did not. Sources with fewer than 3 rows are folded into “Other”.

On-criteria drift

The ledger files off-criteria submissions into their own section rather than tagging them per row, so this is a count of what the automated applicants sent that you would not have chosen.

05 — Everything

All {{N}} ledger rows, searchable, each linked back to its Gmail thread.

Primary source. Job Search/pipeline.md, extracted at publish time by Dashboards/build-data.mjs. Nothing here is fetched at load, so the page is exactly as current as the last publish — the date in the strip above is the ledger's own last-swept date, not this page's.

One source, three files. The ledger was split on 2026-08-06 when pipeline.md passed 292KB and stopped being readable in one pass. pipeline.md holds live work, pipeline-leads.md the un-worked backlog, pipeline-archive.md the closed rows and sweep history. All three are parsed here; the row count is the check that none of them dropped out.

Repeat counts are ledger-only, and they undercount. A second layer used to read the Google Sheet archive for all-time counts through the Drive connector. That was retired on 2026-08-06 and the code is gone — the sheet is a lagging archive that no sweep reads or writes. To check whether a company is a genuine repeat, search Gmail for in:anywhere "<company>"; that covers every application ever made and cannot go stale.
