## 🎯 The goal, the ask, and the step-up line — read this before the tiers

**Three numbers that are easy to collapse into one, and collapsing them makes every below-band role get reported wrong.**

| Number | What it is | What it is for |
|---|---|---|
| **{{DREAM}}** | 🌟 **The dream.** Rare in this market. | Aspiration. Not a plan. |
| **{{GOAL}}** | 🎯 **THE REAL GOAL.** What the search is actually aimed at. | **What "done" looks like.** Survives accepting anything below it. |
| **{{ASK_RANGE}}** *(tier 1)* | ✅ **THE ASK.** What gets quoted to a recruiter. | Negotiating position. |
| **{{STEP_UP}}** | 🟡 **THE STEP-UP LINE.** The number above which a role beats the current situation. | **A private accept/decline aid. NEVER a number to quote.** |

🔴 **THE MOST USEFUL VERSION OF THE GOAL IS A NUMBER ALREADY EARNED, NOT A NUMBER WANTED.** If {{FIRST_NAME}} has been paid {{PRIOR_COMP}} before, **the goal is a restoration, and that is evidence rather than ambition.** It is the difference between *"here's my range"* and *"here's what my last role in this discipline paid."*

⭐ **AND IT IS A NEGOTIATING ASSET WITH AN ASYMMETRY BUILT IN.** Many US states — California's Labor Code §432.3 among them — **bar an employer from ASKING about salary history, while an applicant may VOLUNTEER it and the employer may then consider it.** So a prior salary is {{FIRST_NAME}}'s to deploy at the moment of choosing, and nobody can pull it out first.

⚠️ **Deploy it when a number is being set, not in a first reply.** It is the strongest single sentence available and it is worth more late than early. ⚠️ **Not legal advice — verify the statute in {{FIRST_NAME}}'s state before relying on it.**

✅ **The same class of statute usually cuts the other way too:** §432.3 requires an employer to provide the **pay scale** on reasonable request from an applicant who has completed an initial interview. 🔴 **That is the answer to "three conversations and nobody has said a number" — ask, in writing, and they must answer.** ⚠️ **Read what comes back carefully:** if a published band already exists because the law required it in the posting, that band is probably the real ceiling rather than an opening position.

### How a below-band requisition gets reported

- ✅ **Report it as what it is: below the ask, above the step-up line, {{FIRST_NAME}}'s call.** Not *"fails the floor, dead."*
- ⚠️ **Give the annualised figure and the gap to goal**, so the trade-off is visible. A role at {{EXAMPLE_LOW}} is a step up *and* short of goal — **both facts belong in the output; reporting only the cheerful one is how a stepping stone quietly becomes a destination.**
- 🔴 **UNCHANGED — what gets quoted.** The step-up line is a private threshold for deciding, never a figure to put in a draft or say on a call. **Quoting near it would price {{FIRST_NAME}} at the fallback instead of the worth, and would make the goal unreachable from inside the job taken.** **Ask high, accept knowingly — those are different acts.**
- 🔴 **UNCHANGED — the hard no-fits.** Location, discipline and seniority are not money and do not bend because a rate clears the step-up line. **A cheap wrong-role req is still a wrong-role req.**
- ✅ **Accepting a lower-paying role does not end the search** unless {{FIRST_NAME}} says it does. A stepping-stone acceptance leaves the ledger open.

## Compensation

**Three separate scales. Pick by engagement type — never quote one tier's number for another tier's work.**

### 1. Full-time salary

**{{TIER1_RANGE}}.** {{TIER1_FLOOR}} is a **hard floor**.

### 2. W2 hourly / agency contract placement

**{{TIER2_RANGE}}.** Salary-equivalent, computed at a 2,080-hour year. **If the salary range changes, recalculate this from it** rather than setting it independently.

This tier is for staffing-agency placements where {{FIRST_NAME}} is a W2 employee of the agency.

### 3. Independent contract / project work

**Up to {{TIER3_CEILING}}.** Scoped per project, on its own scale — **not derived from salary.**

**The premium is not markup.** Independent work bills for time a salaried role absorbs invisibly: client correspondence, scoping calls, project management, revision rounds, and the gaps between engagements. **A project rate that ignores that overhead is a pay cut wearing a bigger number.**

🔴 **No floor is set for this tier. Do not invent one, and do not fall back to the W2 hourly floor** — that would badly underprice project work. **When an independent inquiry needs a number, surface it for {{FIRST_NAME}} to scope rather than drafting a figure.**

### Using these numbers

**Tiers 1 and 2 are authorized figures** — drafted replies may state them directly when a recruiter asks for expectations. **This is the one pricing exception to `../Prompts/reply-tone.md`'s rule against committing to numbers on {{FIRST_NAME}}'s behalf.** 🔴 **Tier 3 is NOT authorized for drafting**; it is context for recognizing a low offer, not a number to quote.

- 🔴 **Identify the tier FIRST.** Full-time role, agency W2 placement, or independent project? **If the JD or email doesn't make it clear, say so and quote nothing.**
- Quote the **range**, not a single number — unless the recruiter demands one figure, then give the midpoint of the applicable range.
- **Don't anchor at the bottom.** A floor is a floor, not an opening offer.
- A posting whose published band tops out under the tier-1 floor gets **flagged, not killed** — see the step-up line above.
- **Never negotiate past these numbers, agree to a specific offer, or discuss equity, bonus, or benefits structure in a draft.** Surface those.
- **If the JD publishes its own band, note how it compares in the output** so {{FIRST_NAME}} sees the gap or overlap before sending anything.

## Project-type preferences

⚠️ **These are RANKING preferences, not hard no-fits.** They never disqualify a requisition and never justify declining one. **What they change is emphasis** — which parts of a role get led with in a reply, and which get left unmentioned.

**More of:** {{PREFERRED_WORK}}

**Less of:** {{DISPREFERRED_WORK}}

🔴 **THE HANDLING RULE, AND IT COMES FROM A REAL MISREAD: OMIT, NEVER DECLINE.**

In the source repo a hiring manager pitched a project type the candidate privately disliked. **The candidate engaged warmly on the call — and said afterwards he had been being polite, not enthusiastic.** The call transcript read as genuine interest and it was not. **A drafted reply built from the transcript alone got it exactly backwards.**

✅ **So: leave the disliked work unmentioned in drafted replies and put the emphasis elsewhere.** ⚠️ **Never draft a paragraph declining a project type** — it answers a question nobody asked, contradicts the candidate's own on-call warmth, and hands an employer a reason to hesitate before an offer exists. ✅ **The right moment to surface a project-mix concern is a scoping or offer conversation, and the natural vehicle is a question about what the role actually owns.**

⚠️ **A capability {{FIRST_NAME}} has but dislikes still gets a clean YES to a recruiter.** Do not hedge it, do not qualify it, and never let a private dislike leak into a drafted reply. **If there is a real professional reason behind the dislike, that reason usually inverts into a selling point** — lead with the process rigour it implies rather than with portfolio volume.

## Role taxonomy → Gmail `Career/*` labels

**Every real opportunity gets exactly one role-type label.** These are **categorization, not stage** — stage lives in `../Job Search/pipeline.md`. **A thread's role type never changes; its stage changes constantly. Don't conflate them.**

⚠️ **Create these labels in Gmail before the first sweep** (`create_label`, or by hand in Gmail settings). **Confirm IDs with `list_labels` on every run** — IDs are not guaranteed stable, and **a stale ID applied automatically files a thread somewhere nobody will look for it.**
